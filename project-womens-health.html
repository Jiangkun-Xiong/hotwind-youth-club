A student discussion designed around who should receive information about pregnancy-related risks, what it should cover and who should communicate it.

The posters and plans record the topics and proposed format. They do not establish attendance, a consensus or an evaluated educational outcome.

What makes health information helpful, balanced and appropriate?

The discussion plan connects pregnancy-related health information with informed choice, audience and responsibility for public education.

Three questions in the discussion plan

01

Who is the audience?

Should information about pregnancy-related risks, responsibilities and possible consequences reach women beyond those currently planning a pregnancy, including students?

02

What should it cover?

How much detail should risk communication provide, and how should its accuracy and presentation be considered?

03

Who should take part?

Should pregnancy-related health knowledge form part of general education for women and men, especially students, and to what extent?

My contribution

I helped organise and facilitate the student discussion with the team, focusing on health communication and medical ethics.

WHAT I TOOK FROM IT

These are questions for discussion. The page does not state a group consensus or provide individual medical advice.
